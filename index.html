  About the Workshop

What is HydroImaging?

A focused full-day workshop at the crossroads of image processing, remote sensing, and environmental science.

Earth Observation is a rapidly growing research field that combines computer vision, machine learning, and signal/image processing to address some of the most pressing challenges related to climate change, the water cycle, and environmental attribute prediction - including water quality, snow depth, vegetation cover, and pollution.

HydroImaging brings together experts in machine vision, satellite observation, machine learning, remote sensing, hydrology, and land-surface modelling. The workshop fosters collaboration between the Computer Vision, Remote Sensing, and Environmental Monitoring communities, and builds bridges between IEEE technical societies including GRSS, SPS, SSIT, and CIS.

With the rapid advances in multi-modal sensing, large-scale datasets such as Copernicus, and the growing ecosystem of pretrained foundation models, the workshop aims to catalyse new collaborations enabling scalable, observation-driven prediction for operational environmental use.

  Keynote Address

"From Fields to Fires to Fens: Integrating Deep Learning and Multi-Sensor Earth Observation for Global Environmental Resilience"

Prof. Kevin Tansey · University of Leicester, UK

Abstract

The convergence of climate change and population growth necessitates advanced Earth Observation (EO) frameworks to monitor terrestrial ecosystems and ensure food security. This talk explores the evolution of remote sensing methodologies — transitioning from traditional statistical models to sophisticated deep learning architectures — to address three critical environmental pillars: agricultural productivity, wildfire dynamics, and peatland preservation.

In the agricultural domain, the research takes two pathways. The first is a multi-scale deep learning framework integrating time-series satellite data, meteorological variables, and soil characteristics. Using models such as LSTM and neural networks, high-precision county-level yield estimates of crops such as winter wheat are achieved. The second pathway, developed with industry partners in the UK and Nigeria, predicts crop status, harvest windows, and crop type for the determination of crop waste availability for biofuel through a pyrolysis process.

The increasing frequency of extreme weather events has triggered an increase in wildfire activity outside of normal fire seasons. Work on automated processing pipelines using high-resolution PlanetScope and Sentinel imagery to characterise burned area will be discussed. Using adaptive machine learning classifications, 3-metre resolution burned-area maps are generated, enabling rapid response and long-term ecological impact assessment.

An overview of findings from the ESA WorldPeatland project — an international initiative dedicated to EO-based mapping and monitoring of peatland hydrology — will be presented. Peatlands are vital carbon sinks, yet highly vulnerable to drainage and fire. The catalogue of products created by the project team and insights into peatland extent, hydrology, and surface motion will be discussed.

Finally, Prof. Tansey will outline the changing direction of remote sensing and Earth observation research as seen through his role as Editor-in-Chief of the International Journal of Remote Sensing, and the challenges faced by the community regarding the use and misuse of AI in scientific publishing.

About the Speaker

Kevin Tansey is a Geographer who has worked as a remote sensing scientist since 1995. He has published 112 papers in international peer-reviewed journals across application fields including agriculture, wildfire and hazards assessment, forestry, and renewable energies. He works with all forms of EO data — optical, thermal, radar, and LiDAR — from the ground to space.

He is Editor-in-Chief of the International Journal of Remote Sensing. He has previously held positions on the United Nations World Meteorological Organization (WMO) Terrestrial Observations Panel for Climate (TOPC) and has been co-chair of the Committee of Earth Observing Satellites (CEOS) Land Product Validation sub-group on fire disturbance. He has supervised more than 25 PhD students and has over 20 years of teaching experience in remote sensing, GIS, and statistics for data science.

  Call for Papers

Submit Your Research

We welcome contributions on methods, applications, and open-data initiatives at the frontier of imaging and environmental science.

  Topics of Interest

Methods & Models

  • Data-centric machine learning for remote sensing data
  • Language and vision models (LLMs, CLIP, Visual Language Models)
  • Data assimilation for image/temporal resolution discrepancy
  • Multi-modal fusion for parameter estimation and prediction
  • Generative models (GANs, diffusion) for remote sensing
  • Explainability and interpretability of environmental AI
  • Super-resolution and image reconstruction

Applications

  • Disaster relief and flood detection & mapping
  • Snow and water prediction
  • Coast, sea, and marine monitoring
  • Pollution and air/water quality analysis
  • Sustainable and intelligent agriculture
  • Climate change and geoscience applications
  • Urban planning and phenological studies

Open Data & Benchmarks

  • New open datasets for remote sensing and hydrology
  • Annotated imaging datasets for environmental analysis
  • Reproducible pipelines and evaluation benchmarks
